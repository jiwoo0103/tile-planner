## The purchase in one view

For the example below, **45 required tiles become 12 full boxes containing 48 tiles**. At a hypothetical **$32.50 USD per box**, the tile subtotal is **$390.00**. The difference of 3 tiles comes from box rounding; it is not a forecast of intact tiles left after installation.

This is an original worked calculation for an imagined rectangular room and product. The dimensions, pack size, allowance and price are teaching inputs, not a product listing, current quotation or installation recommendation.

## Set the room and product inputs

Use these exact values to follow along:

- **Room width:** 3.5 m.
- **Room length:** 4.2 m.
- **Tile width and length:** 60 cm × 60 cm, equivalent to 0.6 m × 0.6 m.
- **Waste allowance:** 10%, chosen only to demonstrate the calculation.
- **Tiles per box:** 4; assume this example product is purchased in full boxes.
- **Price per box:** $32.50 USD, a hypothetical price created for this example.

[Open this purchase example in the calculator](/calculator/?roomWidth=3.5&roomWidthUnit=m&roomLength=4.2&roomLengthUnit=m&tileWidth=60&tileWidthUnit=cm&tileLength=60&tileLengthUnit=cm&waste=10&tilesPerBox=4&pricePerBox=32.50). All measurements, units, allowance, pack quantity and price are filled in. You can change them to your own values later.

For a real order, establish the measured area first using the [room measuring guide](/guides/how-to-measure-a-room-for-tile/). Confirm the exact product's dimensions, pieces per box and ordering unit with the supplier. A price per square foot or per tile cannot be entered unchanged into the calculator's **price per box** field.

## Calculate area and the base tile count

The room area is **3.5 × 4.2 = 14.7 m²**. Each tile has an area of **0.6 × 0.6 = 0.36 m²**. Dividing the two gives the unrounded area ratio:

**14.7 ÷ 0.36 = 40.8333… tile areas.**

The calculator separately rounds that ratio up to **41 base tiles**. This is the area-only count before allowance. It does not establish whether particular edge cuts can be made from those tiles. Grout and reuse of offcuts are not deducted from this purchase estimate.

Keep the full area ratio for the next step. Do not use the displayed, already rounded base count as the input to the allowance calculation.

## Apply allowance, then round to required tiles

A 10% allowance means multiplying the unrounded area ratio by 1.10:

**(14.7 ÷ 0.36) × 1.10 = 44.9166… → 45 required tiles.**

The arrow means round up to the next whole tile. The allowance and tile rounding together increase the whole-tile estimate from 41 base tiles to 45 required tiles, a difference of 4. That difference is not exactly 10% of 41: the percentage applies to the unrounded ratio.

If you instead multiply 41 × 1.10, you get 45.1 and would round to 46. That is the early-rounding mistake this calculator avoids. Its shared [calculation method](/calculator/#calculation-method) uses the first formula throughout.

The 10% input does not decide how much this particular installation should allow. Cuts, breakage and planned repair stock need a job-specific review; the [waste allowance guide](/guides/tile-waste-allowance/) explains those separate purposes.

## Round to full boxes and purchased tiles

With 4 tiles in each box, **45 ÷ 4 = 11.25 boxes**. Under the full-box assumption, round up to **12 boxes**. Rounding down to 11 would buy only **11 × 4 = 44 tiles**, one short of the 45-tile planning requirement.

The purchase therefore contains **12 × 4 = 48 tiles**. Two different comparisons now matter:

- **Box surplus:** 48 purchased − 45 required = **3 tiles** added by whole-box rounding.
- **Above the base count:** 48 purchased − 41 base = **7 tiles**, comprising the 4-tile difference introduced by allowance and tile rounding, plus 3 from box rounding.

Do not add the 10% allowance again to the 48 purchased tiles: it is already included in the required quantity used to select the boxes.

## Separate box surplus from actual leftovers

The calculator's 3 extra tiles are a difference between two planned quantities. They are not 3 guaranteed repair spares, and the 7 tiles above the base count are not 7 tiles that will necessarily be discarded.

If the job hypothetically consumed exactly 45 source tiles, the 48-tile purchase would leave 3 intact tiles. Actual consumption may differ because of cuts, reusable offcuts, breakage and the selected layout. Only checking the usable stock after the job establishes the actual leftovers. If you want a particular repair reserve, agree how it is included in the order with your installer.

The [2D layout preview](/layout/) can show straight-grid edge pieces, but its placed-piece counts are not a cutting optimization or a prediction of leftover source tiles. The [starting-point guide](/guides/tile-layout-starting-point/) compares corner and centered grids with exact edge dimensions.

## Work out the tile budget

For this example only, multiply **12 boxes × $32.50 per box = $390.00 USD**. Budget for the whole purchase, including box surplus, because the assumed sale unit is a full box.

The price is entirely hypothetical and is not taken from a supplier. The subtotal excludes tax, delivery, installation, adhesive, grout, trim, tools and any other charges. It is a tile-material subtotal, not a complete renovation budget.

The calculator should show:

| Result | Example value |
| --- | --- |
| Floor area | 14.7 m² |
| Base tiles | 41 tiles |
| Required tiles | 45 tiles |
| Full boxes | 12 boxes |
| Purchased tiles | 48 tiles |
| Box surplus | 3 tiles |
| Tile subtotal | $390.00 USD |

## Check the order before buying

Replace the hypothetical inputs with the selected product's information. Confirm the box contents, supplier-stated coverage, sale unit and actual box price. This calculation uses tile dimensions directly; if the supplier's stated coverage differs, resolve the reason before relying on a quantity. Check return conditions and the desired spare stock rather than assuming that surplus pieces are returnable.

Have the final quantity reviewed against the installation. As one manufacturer example, [Fireclay Tile's ordering FAQ](https://www.fireclaytile.com/a/faqs) says final quantities must be verified by the installer, architect or contractor. Its return policy also specifies original packaging and full boxes for tile returns. Those are Fireclay's terms, not a policy for every seller or for this imagined product.

Keep the distinction on your order notes: **45 required, 12 boxes ordered, 48 tiles purchased, 3 extra from box rounding, $390.00 hypothetical tile subtotal**. The calculator gives a reproducible starting estimate; your product and installation checks determine the final order.
